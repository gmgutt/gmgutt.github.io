How to work with George.

Working together

Lead with kindness and authenticity

Skip the corporate pleasantries and posturing. Engage with him through direct, honest communication and genuine critical thinking. Say what you actually think. Raise concerns early. Disagree openly when you see a better path. Treat colleagues with the same respect he extends to his own team.

Working together

Focus on systemic solutions

When something breaks, bring him an analysis of the workflow failure rather than a list of personal errors. The failure might be a deployment, a missed deadline, a defect in production, or a handoff between teams. Explain what in the process allowed it to happen. Propose a change that keeps it from happening again.

Working together

Embrace continuous optimization

Be open to stripping away legacy administrative processes. Also be open to improving any part of how teams build software: requirements and planning, architecture and design, testing and quality, security, cloud infrastructure, DevOps pipelines, operations, and the team’s own ways of working.

What drives him.

Motivation

Why George leads

  • To eliminate developer burnout

    He steps into leadership to dismantle the “Improvement Trap.” In that trap, teams work longer hours instead of fixing systemic process friction.

  • To build psychological safety

    He leads to protect his engineers from corporate blame. That protection gives them the safety to innovate and take calculated architectural risks.

  • To bypass corporate red tape

    He uses his position to shield his teams from unnecessary administrative friction. His teams can then focus on high-value software delivery.

Values

What George values

  • Authentic relationships

    He believes great software architecture starts with high social cohesion among engineers who genuinely care about one another.

  • The “speed of trust”

    He prioritizes deep professional trust over heavy documentation. He sees trust as a hard economic driver that speeds up software cycles.

  • Surgical efficiency

    He values automation and lean workflows that clear a path for developers over performative “hard work.”

Strengths

George’s strengths

  • Surgical process optimization

    George doesn’t believe in the “work harder” myth. His greatest analytical strength is walking into an engineering ecosystem, evaluating a sluggish delivery lifecycle, finding the systemic bottleneck, and automating it out of existence.

  • Cultivating high-trust, safe environments

    He believes that “great technology starts with great relationships.” That belief drives him to create authentic psychological safety. He leads with kindness and strips away corporate blame. As a result, his engineers feel comfortable taking calculated architectural risks. They focus on system fixes rather than defensive cover-ups.

  • No-nonsense pragmatism

    He has zero tolerance for “corporate theater” or manufactured team-building. As an executive, he keeps technical consulting pure and practical. He anchors his team’s bonding in shared critical thinking and real-world problem-solving.

Where friction shows up.

Struggles

George’s struggles

  • Navigating rigid bureaucracies

    He hits a wall in slow-moving enterprise environments where multi-layered change management boards and manual approval gates govern the work.

  • Performative corporate environments

    He struggles in companies that prioritize compliance optics, strict top-down KPIs, or weaponized engineering metrics.

  • Managing hyper-isolated developers

    His relationship-driven model faces friction with “silo” developers who refuse to collaborate or integrate with the team’s shared culture.

Triggers

What pushes George’s buttons

  • “Corporate theater”

    Forced workplace bonding, mandatory compliance icebreakers, and artificial team-building exercises frustrate his preference for real interactions.

  • Blame culture

    He strongly dislikes environments that blame individual engineers for an outage instead of analyzing systemic flaws.

  • Unnecessary bottlenecks

    He dislikes leaders who demand their teams “work harder” or longer instead of letting him automate the broken workflows driving the slowdown.
